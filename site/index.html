Leverage Environment

How favorable current conditions are for holding leverage in SPY, EFA and EEM, relative to each ETF's own history. The core measure is effective volatility: the risk a position held for a month actually carries, combining daily volatility with how daily moves add up over the month. Which ETF to hold is left to your momentum system.

This is a monitoring tool built on approximations, not investment advice. Read Methodology and caveats before relying on any number here. Momentum, breadth and every result in full are in the detail report.

Favorability against drawdowns

Favorability, 0 to 100 (top), above each ETF's drawdown from its highest prior close (bottom), on the same time axis. 100 is the calmest the ETF has been; red lines mark 20, 50 and 80.

Leverage from a volatility budget

The leverage that would hold a position's risk at a chosen budget: implied leverage = budget ÷ effective volatility, where effective volatility = volatility × √VR₂₁, then capped. Values below 1x mean holding part of the position in cash.

Volatility drag

How much a leveraged position held for a month gives up each year, in its typical (compound) outcome, compared with simply multiplying the ETF's own compound return by the leverage: drag ≈ L(L−1)/2 × effective volatility². It grows with the square of volatility, so it is small in calm markets and large in turbulent ones.
